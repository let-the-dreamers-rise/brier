Brier.
Admission layer · Rain protocol · Arbitrum One
Screening for agent-created prediction markets

You cannot resolve your way past a bad question.

Every oracle in this market works at the exit, on a question that already has money on it. Brier works at the door. A market that cannot be settled never opens.

01 — The ceiling

Better resolution has a measured limit, and the question sets it.

These are not our numbers. They are from the most recent evaluation of multi-agent AI oracles — three models, evidence retrieval, confidence-weighted aggregation, run over 1,189 KalshiBench questions.

83.4%
Best accuracy any architecture reached. Deliberation made it worse — 76.1% — as confident models flipped correct ones.
13.8%
164 of 1,189 questions resist correction by any multi-agent approach. The authors call it “a hard core of failures.”
97.9%
Accuracy once you keep only unanimous, high-confidence answers — on 47% of the set. The rest has to go to a human.

Error correlation between models runs r=0.53–0.69, which the authors say places “a fundamental limit on ensemble-based approaches.” Adding models does not clear it. On Companies, Crypto and Economics the best architecture lands at 58–67% and stays there.

So there is a floor of questions that no resolver will ever get right, and it is a property of the question rather than the resolver. The only place that floor can be raised is before the market opens.

02 — The door

Everyone is standing at the exit.

The same paper says what nobody is doing, in its own words:

“No admission-time screening is proposed or implemented … questions fundamentally unsuitable for automated resolution are identified through error analysis post-hoc rather than filtered pre-resolution.”

At the exit — after money is at risk
Crowded and funded
  • Cournot — AI-native oracle, YZi Labs backed, live as the resolution layer for 42
  • UMA — optimistic oracle, settles Polymarket
  • Kleros + Reality.eth — bonded escalation into juror arbitration
  • Tellor, and the published multi-agent architectures
At the door — before a market opens
Nobody
  • Kalshi comes closest — but by lawyers drafting contract terms per market and filing them with the CFTC. Correct, and it cannot scale to markets an agent writes from a prompt.
  • Rain's buildCreateMarketTx has no resolution field at all
  • Polymarket has a resolutionSource field and leaves it empty on 76% of markets

This makes Cournot a customer, not a competitor. A better resolver still inherits whatever question it is handed. Brier improves the input to every one of them — the same way a type checker does not compete with the runtime.

04 — The evidence

$384M of live liquidity sits on markets with no machine-readable source.

This part needs no opinion from anyone. Polymarket publishes a resolutionSource field per market. It either holds a fetchable URL or it does not. Surveyed every open market their public API returns:

node bin/survey.mjs1,192 open markets · $427.0M liquidity
no fetchable resolutionSource   907/1192   76.1%
  as a share of liquidity       $383.9M   89.9%

settles on human judgement      864/1192   72.5%
  as a share of liquidity       $295.5M   69.2%

// "human judgement" = the description defers to phrasing like
// "a consensus of credible reporting" instead of an instrument.

Nine of the ten deepest sourceless books are the 2028 Democratic nomination ladder — $2.8M each on Kim Kardashian, MrBeast, Oprah — every one settling on “a consensus.”

Where the grader is weaker than that headlineread before quoting

The survey needs no labelling. The grader's own accuracy is a separate and more modest claim over those same markets: 0.8% refused unrewritably, SUPERLATIVE_UNTIED 8.6%, TEMPORAL_VAGUE 7.0%, VAGUE_PREDICATE 0.8%.

Three caveats. The grader refuses all 1,192 on structural grounds, but that is an artifact and not a finding — Polymarket has no selector or void-branch fields to populate. TEMPORAL_VAGUE over-fires: it flags “before 2027”, precise apart from timezone, so read 7.0% as an upper bound on a minor defect. And the probes are lexical, not semantic; they catch constructs that have empirically caused disputes and will miss a question ambiguous for a reason no probe knows about. A 20-question adversarial corpus in the repo passes 20/20, and that number is circular — one author wrote both labels and probes — so it is a regression test, not evidence.

05 — Why Rain

Creation got automated. Screening did not.

Rain's pitch is that an agent turns a prompt into a live market with no manual coding. Its creation call takes marketQuestion and marketDescription as free text plus one boolean, isPublicPoolResolverAi. There is no field for the source, the instant, the tie-break, or the void branch. Market creation at machine speed, settlement rules still in prose.

Rain, measuredValueReading
Total value locked$100.1M After Rain committed $100M of its own. Capital is solved.
Cumulative volume$142.4M Thin against that liquidity. Volume is the scarce good.
Marquee World Cup market$1.3M The next ten did $40K–$300K each.
Builder revenue share0.5% Flat, on generated volume, paid from the token allocation.

Brier is a platform, not a service sitting outside the flow — markets are created and traded through it on Rain rails, so it generates volume and earns that 0.5%. A flat rebate on volume also invites wash trading, and screening plus receipts makes honest volume legible. It defends the grant budget rather than mining it.

06 — What ships

The mechanism runs. The corpus is the company.

  • DoneResolution contracts. Canonical serialisation, sha256, binding into marketDescription. Order-independent, so two specs meaning the same thing hash the same. src/spec.mjs
  • DoneAdmission grader. Six probes for constructs that admit more than one reading; each names the field that would collapse it. Two force a rewrite. src/grader.mjs
  • DoneSurvey harness. 1,192 live markets measured, cached and reproducible. bin/survey.mjs · 35 tests passing
  • NextAn independently labelled corpus. Questions labelled by someone who is not the author, tied to what actually happened to those markets. This is the only defensible asset here — a lint is cloneable in a weekend, a corpus is not.
  • NextMarket creation on Rain. Screened markets opening through the SDK, each with a public contract and receipt page.

The unanimity gate for settlement is built (src/settle.mjs) and is deliberately not the pitch. That mechanism is published prior art and a funded competitor's core product. It is kept because it is correct, and it is cited rather than claimed.

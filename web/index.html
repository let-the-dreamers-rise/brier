Brier.
Resolution layer · Rain protocol · Arbitrum One
Prediction markets that know when to stop

Every market ships with a resolution contract, or it doesn't ship.

Rain lets an agent turn a prompt into a live market. Brier is the layer that decides whether that market can be settled at all — and refuses to settle it when the evidence disagrees.

01 — The observation

Market creation got automated. Market resolution didn't.

This is the call that creates a market on Rain. It is the whole interface between “an agent had an idea” and “real money is now at risk.”

buildCreateMarketTx — acceptsrain-sdk
  • marketQuestionfree text
  • marketDescriptionfree text
  • isPublicPoolResolverAiboolean
  • startTime / endTimeuint
  • barValuesuint[]
  • baseTokenaddress
Not accepted — anywherethe gap
  • resolutionSourcewhich instrument settles it
  • selectorwhich field is read
  • observedAtat what instant
  • comparatoragainst what threshold
  • tiebreakwhen two readings tie
  • voidIfwhen the question was bad

The entire settlement specification is prose plus one boolean. That is survivable when a careful human writes the rules text and a bonded human panel reads it. It is not survivable at machine speed.

And even the careful version breaks. A governance attack moved Polymarket's Ukraine mineral-deal market from 9% to 100% and settled “Yes” with no agreement in place, on five million UMA cast across three accounts. The Wall Street Journal found that in most disputed markets, more than half the votes came from ten wallets. Traders sued over a resolution in July 2026. Ambiguous events settle 50-50, so a trader who was demonstrably right gets back half their stake.

02 — The instrument

A resolution contract is an observation specification.

Glenn Brier was a meteorologist. In 1950 he published the scoring rule that made weather forecasts falsifiable, and it works because a weather observation names its instrument, its units, and its moment before anyone forecasts anything. A market question deserves the same discipline.

Every market created through Brier carries this, signed. The hash goes on-chain in marketDescription — a field Rain already has — so the rules cannot be reinterpreted after the outcome is known. No protocol change required.

ResolutionSpec · sha256 50b608d4…85dfsigned at creation
{
  "question":    "Top ARC-AGI-3 semi-private score above 15% on 2026-12-31?",
  "source":      "https://arcprize.org/leaderboard",
  "selector":    "$.arc_agi_3.semi_private[0].score",
  "observed_at": "2026-12-31T23:59:59Z",        // UTC, not "end of year"
  "unit":        "percent",
  "comparator":  "> 15.0",
  "tiebreak":    "earliest submission timestamp wins",
  "void_if": [
    "source unreachable for 24h after observed_at",
    "field absent or non-numeric",
    "scoring methodology revised after market open"
  ],
  "fallback":    "abstain"              // never guess
}

03 — The method

Three gates, and only one of them settles anything.

Before open

Admission grading

The spec is dry-run against the live source, then attacked: enumerate the plausible readings of the question and check whether the spec picks out exactly one. If two readings survive, the question is underspecified.

→ Refused, with the defect named
At open

Contract binding

The spec is hashed and written on-chain with the market. Traders price a question whose settlement rule is fixed and public before they take a position — not one that gets interpreted afterwards.

→ Market opens, rules frozen
At oracleEndTime

Abstaining settlement

N independent resolvers execute the same spec — different models, independent fetches. Unanimous settles. Anything less does not settle at all: it abstains and escalates with the evidence attached.

→ Settle, or hold and escalate

04 — The readout

A wrong settlement costs more than a slow one.

This is the settlement gate. The rule below is the one in src/settle.mjs, and the hashes are real sha256 of the bytes shown. The three verdicts are an example panel, not a live fetch. Flip any resolver and watch what Brier does with a split.

Settlement gate · example panelobserved_at 2026-12-31T23:59:59Z

RSV-B read 15.7 · sha256 3303dbbd… YES
RSV-C read 15.7 · sha256 3303dbbd… YES
Settled — unanimous

All three resolvers read the same value from the named source. buildResolveMarketTx is called and a receipt is published: spec hash, source bytes hash, fetch timestamps, every verdict.

The receipt is the point. Whoever lost the trade can recompute the settlement from published bytes without trusting us, the model, or Rain. That is the part a website cannot do and an escrow can.

05 — The measurement

$384M of live liquidity sits on markets with no machine-readable source.

This is the part that needs no opinion from us. Polymarket publishes a resolutionSource field on every market. Either it holds a fetchable URL or it does not. Counting that is a fact about their data, so it cannot be circular.

Surveyed every open market their public API returns, ranked by liquidity:

Nine of the ten deepest books with no fetchable source are the 2028 Democratic nomination ladder — $2.8M each on Kim Kardashian, MrBeast, Oprah — every one of them settling on “a consensus.” The deepest of all, at $3.0M, asks whether the Fed cuts 25bps in September, and names no source at all.

The grader also finds the attack pattern alive and unpatched. It refuses “Iran agrees to end enrichment of uranium by December 31?” on the same unresolvable predicate — agrees — that it fires on the Ukraine minerals market whose odds were driven 9% to 100% and settled Yes with no agreement in place. Same defect, different war, still open, still taking money.

Where the grader is weaker than the headlineread this before quoting it

The survey above needs no labelling. The grader's own accuracy is a separate and more modest claim, and these are its real numbers over the same 1,192 markets:

refused unrewritably     9/1192   0.8%   // no spec field can save these
SUPERLATIVE_UNTIED     102      8.6%
TEMPORAL_VAGUE          83      7.0%
VAGUE_PREDICATE          9      0.8%

Three honest caveats. The grader refuses all 1,192 on structural grounds, but that is an artifact and not a finding — Polymarket has no selector or void-branch fields to populate, so the check is trivially true and we do not count it. TEMPORAL_VAGUE over-fires: it flags “before 2027”, which is precise apart from the timezone, so treat that 7.0% as an upper bound on a minor defect. And the probes are lexical, not semantic — they catch constructs that have empirically produced disputes and will miss a question ambiguous for a reason no probe knows about.

The strongest grader result is the narrow one: the VAGUE_PREDICATE cluster is real, it is the same defect that was exploited for money, and it is still live on Polymarket today.

A separate 20-question adversarial corpus, covered by 35 tests, sits in the repo. It passes 20/20, and that number is circular — one author wrote both the labels and the probes — so it is a regression test, not evidence.

Liquidity is not the constraint. Trustworthy market supply is.

Brier is a platform, not an oracle sitting outside the flow. Markets are created and traded through it on Rain rails, so it generates volume and earns that 0.5%.

It also protects it. A flat rebate on volume invites wash trading — agents cycling size against each other to farm the rebate. Receipts make honest volume legible and self-dealing obvious. Brier defends the grant budget instead of mining it.

07 — What ships

Two of these are already done.

  • DoneSpec schema and signer. Canonical serialisation, sha256, and the binding into marketDescription. Order-independent, so two specs that mean the same thing hash the same. src/spec.mjs
  • DoneAdmission grader and settlement gate. Six ambiguity probes, the unanimity rule, and the escalation packet. A resolver that could not read is counted as a hold, never a vote. src/grader.mjs, src/settle.mjs
  • NextAn independently labelled corpus. Someone other than the author labels 100 questions, so the number above stops being circular.
  • NextLive resolvers. Real fetches against real sources, replacing the example panel above.
  • ThenMarket surface. Creation and trading on the Rain SDK with account abstraction, and a public receipt page per market.

Launch vertical is AI outcomes — model releases, benchmark scores, competition results — because those sources are machine-readable, which makes the spec discipline provable on day one instead of aspirational.
